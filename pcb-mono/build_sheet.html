NK Smart Pumps — monoblock board v2

Design sheet · single-phase monoblock, 1.5 HP maximum · the paid submersible board is a different product
Two products. The board already ordered (small relay, external contactor) stays the submersible controller. This sheet is the monoblock controller: the motor live and neutral land on this board, and there is no KM contactor in the box.
Rating printed on the board: 1.5 HP, 230 V, single phase, monoblock only. A 2 HP motor can start near the relay’s limit. A submersible still uses the first board and its contactor, because the submersible control box expects that arrangement.

1. The relay that actually switches the pump

The part is a TE Connectivity T92, two normally-open poles, silver-cadmium-oxide contacts, 5 V DC coil, PCB pins. Search T92P7D12-5. Before buying, the label must say 5 VDC, 2 form A (two NO), and printed-circuit terminals. A 12 V coil, a quick-connect panel relay, or a 16 A smart-plug relay does not fit this design.

With a DC coil, each pole is rated 18 A running (FLA) and 110 A start (LRA) at 240 V, for 100,000 operations. A 1.5 HP single-phase monoblock is about 10 A running and about 60–70 A at the moment it starts. One pole switches live. The other switches neutral, so the pump is dead when the relay is open. The datasheet’s “3 HP” line is only 1,000 operations, so this product is not sold as 3 HP.

The 1 A fuse is on the HLK branch only. Pump current never goes through it, so a normal start does not blow the fuse and generate a “board is dead” return.

3. Power and the coil driver

The supply is an HLK-10M05 (5 V, 2 A). The coil holds 0.34 A, and the ESP32 Wi-Fi peak sits on top of that, about 0.9 A together. The 1 A module used on the first board is too small for this coil and would reset the controller.

A BC547 is a 100 mA transistor, so it cannot drive this coil. The driver is a logic-level N-channel MOSFET, IRLZ44N or any part that is fully on at 3.3 V and rated at least 1 A. GPIO32, active high, is unchanged from the first board. A 100 kΩ resistor from the gate to ground holds the pump off while the ESP32 boots. A 1N4007 across the coil (cathode to the 5 V side) absorbs the turn-off spike. A 1000 µF, 16 V capacitor sits on the 5 V rail next to the driver.

4. Selector and lamp

The door selector switches the coil (0.34 A), never the motor. Use a 3-pole, 3-position switch rated at least 6 A. The third pole only tells the ESP32 which position is selected, so the phone shows AUTO, MANUAL, or OFF.

MAN5 V connected to the coil topCoil bottom connected to groundThe coil is across 5 V by itself. The pump runs with the ESP32 unplugged.

Wire it as: pole 1 common to the board +5V terminal; pole 1 AUTO and pole 1 MAN linked together to the board COIL terminal; pole 2 common to the board GND terminal; pole 2 MAN to the board MAN terminal. Leave pole 2 AUTO and OFF empty. Pole 3 common to the board GND as well; pole 3 AUTO to SA; pole 3 MAN to SM. SA and SM are 3.3 V sense wires with a 1 kΩ resistor in series. In the sketch set MONO_PCB 1. The app subscribes to selector and shows the word AUTO, MANUAL, or OFF. That line is the door switch. The existing Auto mode switch in the app is still the software auto mode.

The lamp is a 230 V indicator across the motor output (after the relay), so it lights only when the pump actually has power.

5. The breaker, the snubber, and a welded contact

Fit a motor protection breaker, not only a 16 A C-curve MCB. A stalled 1.5 HP motor can sit at 50–70 A. A C16 often waits for 80–160 A before it trips quickly, so the motor can burn. Use a motor breaker set to the nameplate current, about 9–14 A (Schneider GV2ME16 or an equivalent), in the supply ahead of this board.

Across the MOTOR screws, after the relay, fit a snubber: 0.1 µF X2, 275 VAC, in series with a 100 Ω, 2 W, flameproof resistor. It is wired on the motor terminals, not as a part on the board. That reduces the arc that welds the contacts. A failed capacitor loads the line only while the pump is commanded on, and the motor breaker can open it. Do not put a MOV across the motor. The small 14D471 surge part, if fitted, stays on the HLK side of the 1 A fuse only.

The app cannot open a contact that has welded. Dry-run protection works only while the relay still releases. Open the motor breaker before working on the pump. Never plug USB in while 230 V is on the board.

7. Board fabrication rules

  • 200 × 164 mm, 2 layers, 2 oz copper. Motor tracks are 4.0 mm, which on 2 oz copper carries the 10 A running current. The last few millimetres into each screw neck down to 1.5 mm so the round end of a wide track does not touch the next screw. The HLK branch is 1.2 mm and is the only copper through the 1 A fuse.
  • 230 V copper stays at least 8 mm from 5 V and 3.3 V copper, on the same layer and through the board. The ground pour stays 13 mm clear of the nearest motor copper. A milled slot under the relay separates its contact pins from its coil pins. Keep that slot.
  • Mains and motor connectors are Phoenix MKDS 3 (24 A, 2.5 mm²). The smaller 1.5 mm² blocks are only for the sensor, the selector, and the lamp.
  • KiCad’s design-rule check on this file reports no errors and no unconnected pads. Gerbers are in pcb-mono\out. The parts list is pcb-mono\out\assembly\mono_controller_bom.csv and the placement file is mono_controller_cpl.csv in the same folder.
  • The box is vented. The relay (1.7 W in the coil, plus contact heat) and the HLK are not packed in foam.
Do not upload this board until the relay is checked on paper. The six hole positions follow the TE drawing (contact rows 16.00 mm, contact columns 7.85 mm, coil row 25.40 mm past the near contacts, coil pair 4.70 mm). The coil pair is centered on the contact pair. Print the footprint at 1:1, set a T92P7D12-5 on it, and confirm all six pins drop into the holes before any order.
