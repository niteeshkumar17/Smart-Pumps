1. How it works

The sensor on the tank lid measures the distance down to the water surface about ten times a second. The ESP32 takes the median of nine readings, converts the distance to a percentage using two calibration distances (tank full and tank empty), and decides whether the pump should run. It drives a small relay, the relay switches the coil of a contactor, and the contactor switches the pump.

Every safety decision is made inside the ESP32, so the pump stays protected if the phone, Wi-Fi or internet is down. If the ESP32 itself fails, the AUTO/OFF/MAN selector lets you run the pump by hand.

No float switches in this build. Overflow and dry-run protection rely entirely on the ultrasonic sensor and the firmware: the 95 % cut-off, the sensor-fault stop, the max-run limit and the dry-run check. Keep the sensor face clean and check the reading against the tank regularly. In MAN position nothing stops the pump automatically.

3. Installation layout

Only the sensor (and its small junction box) goes on the tank. All electronics and mains parts sit in one box on a wall at ground level, where they are easy to reach and out of the sun.

  • Run the CAT6 sensor cable at least 30 cm away from the pump cable and any mains wiring. Cross them at right angles if they have to meet.
  • Bring every cable into a box from below through a cable gland, with a drip loop, so water can't run along the cable into the box.
  • Keep the sensor cable at 10 m or less. Use a conduit or UV-rated cable on the roof.
  • Mount the control box above any flood level, out of direct rain and sun, and within good Wi-Fi range (check signal strength on your phone at that spot).

Sensor cable: CAT6 pair assignment

CAT6 has 8 wires in 4 twisted pairs. Doubling up power and ground keeps the voltage drop negligible, and twisting TX with a ground wire reduces noise. Join the sensor leads to the CAT6 in the tank junction box with screw terminals or solder and heat-shrink.

5. Mains wiring (230 V)

Danger: 230 V can kill. If you are not trained, have a licensed electrician do this section. Switch off and lock out the supply before opening the box. Never work on it with wet hands or in a wet area.
Using the existing DB. The box is fed from the MCB that already supplies the pump in your house DB, and that MCB is also how you isolate the box. Before wiring, check two things in the DB: the MCB rating suits the pump (about 10 A C-curve up to 1 HP, 16 A for 1–1.5 HP), and whether an RCCB or ELCB (30 mA) protects that circuit. If there isn't one, adding one in the DB is the single most important safety upgrade for a pump.
  • Order: the pump's existing MCB in the house DB → the box → two branches: (1) F1 fuse, which feeds both the HLK-5M05 and the control circuit (S1, relay contact, contactor coil, lamp), and (2) the contactor main contacts → pump.
  • Contactor: use two of the MNX-9's three main poles, one for live and one for neutral, so the pump is fully isolated when it's off. MNX-9 coil terminals are A1 (from the control circuit) and A2 (to neutral).
  • Submersible pump: single-phase submersibles come with a control box (starter and capacitors, often with an ON/OFF switch and overload). Connect the contactor's output to that control box's L and N input. Leave the control box's wiring to the pump as it is, and leave its own switch ON so the contactor does the switching.
  • Surface motor / monoblock pump: connect the contactor's output directly to the motor's L and N terminals (its run capacitor stays as it is on the motor).
  • Pump size: MNX-9 and 1.5 mm² cable cover single-phase pumps up to 1 HP. Many submersibles are 1–1.5 HP; for those, use an 18 A contactor (MNX-18) and 2.5 mm² cable.
  • Cables: 1.5 mm² copper for the pump circuit (up to 1 HP), 0.75–1 mm² for the control circuit, with ferrules on every stranded end and labels on L, N and E.
  • Earth: connect the pump body, the DIN rail and any metal in the box to the earth terminal. Check that the house earth is good; earthing (and any RCCB) relies on it.

7. Sensor mounting and calibration

  • Mount the sensor facing straight down and level, through a hole in the lid with a cable gland. A small hood or cap above it stops condensation dripping onto the face.
  • Keep the sensor face at least 25–30 cm above the 100 % line. The A02YYUW's blind zone is only 3 cm, but splashes and condensation near the top cause false readings.
  • Keep it at least 20 cm from the walls, the inlet pipe and the overflow pipe. Aim it away from the splash of the inlet.
  • Fix the sensor in its final position before calibrating. Moving it changes both calibration distances.

Level formula

level % = (D_empty − D) / (D_empty − D_full) × 100, then clamp to 0–100

Worked example. The water can be 1.2 m deep between the 0 % and 100 % lines, and the sensor face is 30 cm above the 100 % line. So D_full = 300 mm and D_empty = 300 + 1200 = 1500 mm. If the sensor reads 900 mm: (1500 − 900) / (1500 − 300) × 100 = 50 %.

Calibration steps

StepAction
1Fix the sensor in its final position, power the system, and open the Arduino Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. Each line shows dist=… mm.
2D_full: when the tank is at the level you call 100 % (just below the overflow), note the distance. Or measure it with a tape from the sensor face down to that line.
3D_empty: you don't need to drain the tank. D_empty = D_full + the depth between your 100 % line and your 0 % line (keep 0 % a little above the outlet pipe).
4Enter both values as D_FULL_MM and D_EMPTY_MM, upload again, and check the reading against a tape at two or three levels.

8. Firmware

The full sketch is in firmware/water_tank_controller/water_tank_controller.ino and printed in Appendix A. It compiles for the ESP32 board package 2.x and 3.x; it was test-compiled on core 3.3.8 with PubSubClient 2.8.

Safety rules

ConditionAction
Fewer than 5 valid readings in 2.5 sPump OFF (sensor fault). Clears itself when readings return.
Level ≥ 95 %Pump OFF and the manual request is cancelled
Pump has run longer than 45 minPump OFF, MAX-RUN fault latched until RESET
Level rose less than 2 % in any 5 min of runningPump OFF, DRY-RUN fault latched until RESET
Auto mode, level ≤ 20 %Pump ON until 95 %
Manual ON from the appPump ON until 95 %, a fault, or an OFF command
MQTT connection lostManual request cancelled; auto mode keeps working locally
Pump stopped less than 60 s ago, or just powered upA start waits until 60 s have passed (protects the motor)
Firmware hangsWatchdog resets the ESP32 after 30 s; the relay is OFF during the reset

An OFF command from the app stops the pump. In auto mode it starts again the next time the level falls to 20 %. To keep it off, switch auto mode OFF, or turn S1 to OFF.

Uploading

  1. Install Arduino IDE 2. In Boards Manager install esp32 by Espressif Systems. In Library Manager install PubSubClient by Nick O'Leary.
  2. Open water_tank_controller.ino and fill in the USER SETTINGS: Wi-Fi, broker host and login, TOPIC, DEVICE_ID and the calibration values.
  3. Select board ESP32 Dev Module and your COM port, then Upload. Unplug the 5 V VIN supply while the board is on USB.
  4. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. You should see one status line about every second.

9. Broker and phone app setup

HiveMQ Cloud (free)

  1. Sign up at hivemq.com/cloud and create a free Serverless cluster.
  2. Under Access Management, create two logins with publish and subscribe permission: tank-device for the ESP32 and tank-phone for the app.
  3. Copy the cluster URL (like abc123.s1.eu.hivemq.cloud). The port is 8883 with TLS. Put the URL, tank-device and its password in the sketch, with USE_TLS 1.

Prefer a local broker? Run Mosquitto on a Raspberry Pi or Home Assistant, set USE_TLS 0 and port 1883, and use a VPN (for example Tailscale) for remote access. Never forward router ports to the ESP32.

12. Safety and maintenance

  • With no float switches, the sensor is the only thing that sees the water. Once a month, check the app's level against the real tank level and look at the sensor face for drops, dirt or insects.
  • If the pump circuit has an RCCB or ELCB in the DB, press its test button once a month. If it doesn't trip, replace it. If there is none, consider adding one.
  • A submersible is cooled by the water around it, so running dry damages it within minutes. The firmware stops the pump if the tank level doesn't rise for 5 minutes. If your borewell often runs low, ask your pump dealer about the dry-run protection available for your submersible's control box.
  • Every few months check the cable glands, the sensor face (condensation, insects, spider webs) and the tank junction box.
  • If the electronics fail, S1 in MAN runs the pump directly. Nothing stops it automatically in MAN, so stay nearby and switch back to OFF before the tank overflows.
  • Change the default passwords and topic names before going live. Anyone who knows your broker login and topics can switch your pump.
